# Pitch

**One-liner:** DUPR tells you what you are. Rally decides who you should play tonight, and fills the court when someone bails.

## The problem

Pickleball courts in Utah are packed, but open play is chaos. A 4.0 gets stuck with two beginners, has a bad night and doesn't come back. A bad game costs the facility a regular. When someone cancels an hour before a session, that spot usually sits empty.

## What Rally does

Rally sells **match quality** instead of court time.

1. **Onboarding from a 60-second clip.** A player films a bit of their game. Rally reads their play style (kitchen-line time, drive vs. dink, movement, ready position) and a rough starting skill range. If they have a DUPR rating, Rally uses that for skill.
2. **Balanced sessions.** For each session, Rally puts the night's players onto courts and teams by skill *and* play-style fit, and shows a fairness score per court.
3. **Premium pricing.** Facilities sell balanced sessions at a premium and set rules in plain English ("Tuesday 7pm is 3.5+ only, $12 peak").
4. **Backfill.** When someone cancels, Rally offers the spot to the best-fit player on the waitlist. They reply YES, pay, and the court is full again.
5. **Ratings that get better.** Results from every Rally session update each player's rating (OpenSkill), so the first estimate from the video stops mattering after one night.

## Who pays

The facility. Rally is the commerce layer on top of the booking software they already use (CourtReserve and similar).

## Why it's commerce

- Higher price per session (balanced sessions sell at a premium).
- Recovered revenue from cancellations.
- Retention: players who get good games come back.

## Holy War tie-in (optional)

Rivalry Night: the same engine runs a Cougars vs. Utes session, still balanced on skill, with prices that move with the score of the game.
