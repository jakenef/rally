# Play styles: should similar players play together?

Researched 2026-11-06. **Short answer: there's no real research.** No studies found on pickleball, tennis or badminton doubles pairing; what exists is coaching advice and rec-player opinion. It splits depending on the question.

## Partners (same side of the net): sources disagree

- Complementary: a power player should pair with someone strong in the soft game, to cover more situations. [Pickleball Guru](https://thepickleballguru.com/how-to-choose-the-best-pickleball-partner-for-doubles-success/)
- Similar: two control-focused players "often sync faster" than a banger with a resetter. [Pickleball Streets](https://pickleball-streets.com/skills-and-mastery/doubles-and-partner-synergy/)
- Data: check your own win rate by partner; it varies by person and by opponent. [Recace](https://recace.com/blog/pickleball-doubles-partner-stats-win)
- Outside sport, people say they want a complementary partner but actually choose similar ones (Dijkstra and Barelds 2008, romantic partners; doesn't transfer to sport). [Study](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/147470490800600406)

## Opponents (across the net): sources agree the mismatch hurts

- The most common rec-play complaint is bangers wrecking the slow, strategic game dinkers came for: "no soft game, no strategy — just nonstop power." [Pickleball Union](https://pickleballunion.com/pickleball-bangers-vs-dinkers-strategy-guide/), [pickleball.com](https://pickleball.com/culture/are-bangers-the-worst-people-to-play-pickleball-with)
- Whether bangers are bad for the sport is disputed ([The Kitchen](https://thekitchenpickle.com/blogs/community/why-pickleball-bangers-are-mostly-good-for-the-sport)). That the mismatch makes games less fun isn't.

## What Rally does (spec 030, 020)

1. **Skill balance first.** Everyone agrees on this.
2. **Mirror rule.** Both teams on a court get the same style mix. Two bangers vs. two dinkers is the worst court. One of each per side, or four of a kind, are both fine. This takes no side in the partner debate.
3. **Tempo preference.** Players choose "soft", "fast" or "either", and Rally doesn't mix soft and fast players on a court when it can avoid it.
4. **Later:** learn which partner pairings win and get rebooked, from game history.

## For the pitch

The 52 and 91 in scene 4 come from our own fairness formula, not a study. Say the rule comes from what rec players consistently complain about, and results tune it over time. If asked "is there research?": no good research exists, which is why Rally learns from results instead of guessing.
